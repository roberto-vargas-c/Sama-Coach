01 · Why I built it

I hurt my back. Everyone said: try yoga.

Alone, I couldn’t tell if I was doing it right. So I built the coach I needed.

HypothesisA camera that checks you and a voice that guides you can coach a beginner safely at home.

02 · What it does

Four things a teacher would do

Each feature answers one question a beginner has, from “am I doing this right?” to “what should I practise next?”.

Check alignment

The camera measures every joint

Pick one of 165 poses. The reference figure shows the shape from the best side of the mat; the live camera compares your joints with it and says what to fix first.

Plan with me

One question at a time

A guided intake asks what you want, how long you have and what hurts, then offers three practices to choose from.

Review my checks

Scores, strengths and what to fix first

The coach reads your measured angles and hold times, gives a score out of 100, and plans a next practice that targets the gaps.

LLM as a judge

A second model grades the first

Every plan is scored for safety, fit, sequencing and balance by a separate call. The best one is marked “Judge’s pick”.

Guided practice

A calm voice guides every pose

Sama speaks each pose and the movement in between, adds wrist care before Down Dog, and puts your practice days in the calendar.

03 · Data

Cleaning the pose library

The coach is only as good as its reference poses and its voice. Most of the work went into making that data right.

  • Sources: OpenAI for the coach, MoveNet and BlazePose for body points, my own recordings for every voice line, Firebase and Google Calendar for accounts and practice days.
  • Inconsistent poses: hands ahead of the feet, poses off the mat. Fixed by drawing the correct shape and solving it into 3D.
  • Impossible values: a drawing flattening the shoulders. The solver keeps a minimum body depth.
  • Uneven audio: silences capped and loudness normalised.
3.3 → 0.3 LULoudness spread across the voice clips, before and after remastering. Silence, not volume, made the voice feel broken.

04 · Review tools

Small tools to check everything by eye

165 poses, 320 transitions and 333 clips are too much to check inside the app, so I built a review page for each.

Look→Mark what’s wrong→Fix→Re-check everything

Poses · Pose Review

Every pose, three views, joints you can drag

Each of the 165 poses from three sides: the view the app opens with, a second view along the mat and a three-quarter view between them, with the skeleton drawn on (orange left, teal right) and its alignment rules measured on the reference. Click a figure to open it large and drag any joint; the figure follows, and the corrected joints are saved with a “Wrong” verdict so the pose can be fixed in the app. A wrong pose can also get up to two photos of how it should look; the joints are found in the photos and put on the figure. Reports people send from the app arrive live under “Reported in the app”, with the view they were looking at, and are marked handled once fixed.

Poses · Pose Fixer

Show the fix instead of describing it

Drag a joint and the limb follows, from the front, side or back of the mat. The drawing is then solved into a 3D pose.

Poses · Image to pose

From a photo to the figure in one step

MoveNet finds 17 joints in a photo, on the device, and the figure is moved onto them. “Matched 17 of 17 joints”, then fine-tune and send.

Transitions · Transition Review

Watch every move, loop by loop

Each pose into Mountain and back, as the app plays it. Pause on the bad frame and say which body part is off.

Transitions · Transition Lab

Why moves looked wrong, measured

155 transitions scored headless. The largest one-frame jump went from 86 cm to 7 cm.

Transitions · Inverse kinematics

Planted feet stay planted

Hip and planted foot are fixed; two knees fit. The solver stays near the last frame, leans into the natural bend, and eases into the end pose. Knees no longer snap across.

Transitions · Transition Studio

Pause, drag, and the move bends

Drag the joints at the moment that looks wrong; it becomes a keyframe and the motion bends smoothly through it.

05 · Movement

How the figure moves from pose to pose

A transition is not a cross-fade. It is a route through the body’s positions, a 3D motion with hands and feet that stay put, and a clock taken from real classes.

1 · The route

First, where the body has to go

Every pose belongs to a posture: standing, folded, squatting, seated, lunging, in plank, kneeling and so on. The postures form a graph, joined only where a body can really move from one to the other. A shortest-path search (Dijkstra) finds the way from any pose to any other.

  • Costs shape the choice. A jump back or forward costs extra unless the pace is Quick, and rising from kneeling to standing costs more than folding forward, so routes stay the ones a teacher would cue.
  • Changing sides goes through the family’s symmetric pose first, for example Warrior II right to Mountain, then out to the left.
  • Arm sweeps are added where classes use them: a swan dive from arms overhead into a fold, and a sweep of the arms out to the side, only when the feet are together.

2 · The motion

Limbs swing, they don’t slide

Both poses are placed in the mat’s 3D frame. Each limb then rotates around its joint along the shortest arc (spherical interpolation), so an arm going overhead sweeps out in front instead of passing through the chest.

  • A turn on the spot rotates the whole body around the hips.
  • Hips flex forward: a thigh never swings round behind the body.
  • The head follows the spine a beat later; the arms linger a moment before they move.
  • The camera glides from one pose’s best view to the next.

3 · Planted contacts

What touches the mat stays there

Hands, feet and knees that are on the mat in both poses are pinned. Every frame, two-bone inverse kinematics re-solves the elbow or knee so the hand or foot does not drift.

  • A knee only folds one way: the solver uses the joint’s hinge axis, and when a leg is straight at one end it follows the thigh’s swing, so knees open outward instead of crossing.
  • A foot that must travel steps: the body shifts first, the foot lifts about a third of the way in, travels on a low arc and lands.
  • Nothing sinks into the mat or floats above it.

4 · The clock

07 · Conclusion

Hypothesis: mostly supported

The camera, the voice and the AI plans work together, and the judge makes the plans better. Data quality, not the model, decided how good the coach felt.

Not proven yet: other bodies and other rooms.

Still open

  • Can my recorded motion teach smoother transitions?
  • Does the camera check work for every body?
  • Does cycle-aware practice help people keep going?
